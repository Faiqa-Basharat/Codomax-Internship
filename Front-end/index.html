WHAT IS PROJECTMATCH?

Connecting student ideas with the people ready to build them.

Every semester, students think of great software concepts, campus tools, and design projects. But too often, ideas stall because students lack a specific skill — like UI design or frontend code — or wonder if anyone actually needs what they want to make.
ProjectMatch provides a relaxed, low-pressure space to test student concepts, assess campus interest, and team up with peers across different fields before investing weeks of work.

01 · Explore Concepts

Discover what peers are creating

Browse initiatives proposed by students across computer science, design, and other majors. No finished product required — even early notes or problem briefs are welcomed.

02 · Validate Direction

See if it is okay to go with it

Find out whether fellow students resonate with the problem before writing hundreds of lines of code. Gauge community interest, discuss feasibility, and decide if the concept has legs.

03 · Practical Experience

Turn skills into a portfolio piece

Find a project that matches what you want to practice — whether you're learning HTML/CSS, experimenting with Figma, or structuring accessible workflows for real users.

STUDENT IDEA FRAMEWORK

How to know if a student project is ready to build

Before launching into deep development, run your concept through these four practical checkpoints:

Real Campus Friction

Does the idea address a genuine headache for students — such as finding study spots, club updates, accessibility info, or notes?

Single-Semester Scope

Can a lean team of 2–3 students build a functional prototype within 8–12 weeks without burning out during exams?

Complementary Skill Split

Are tasks clearly divided so designers focus on UI/UX while developers build the interface, giving everyone ownership?

Clear Learning Outcome

Will every collaborator finish the project with practical, demonstrable experience they can talk about in interviews?
